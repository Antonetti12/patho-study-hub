Chapter 18 Practice Test

Chapter 18 practice questions. This test has no official instructor key, so a score is not calculated. Suggested answers stay hidden until you open “Show suggested answer” on a question.

Official-key items are scored automatically after you submit. Suggested answers were written by the site owner’s study assistant, not the instructor. They stay hidden until you open “Show suggested answer” on that question and are not scored. Verify them against your notes. Incomplete items are left unscored.

Question 1

Which of the following is NOT a function of the kidney?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Production of albumin

The liver, not the kidney, produces albumin; the kidney does regulate fluid, excrete wastes, and activate vitamin D.

Question 2

Which of the following describes the normal flow of urine?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Collecting duct to the renal pelvis to the ureter to the bladder

Urine flows from the collecting duct to the renal pelvis, then the ureter, then the bladder (and finally the urethra).

Question 3

Which of the following increases glomerular filtration rate?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Increased hydrostatic pressure in the glomerular capillaries

A higher hydrostatic pressure in the glomerular capillaries increases GFR; afferent constriction or a higher plasma oncotic pressure would lower it.

Question 4

By what process is water reabsorbed from the filtrate?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Osmosis

Water is reabsorbed from the filtrate by osmosis, following solute reabsorption.

Question 5

Which substance directly controls the reabsorption of water from the collecting ducts?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Antidiuretic hormone

ADH (vasopressin) directly controls water reabsorption in the collecting ducts.

Question 6

Under what circumstances do cells in the kidneys secrete renin?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Blood flow in the afferent arteriole decreases.

Juxtaglomerular cells secrete renin when afferent arteriolar blood flow or pressure falls (or when NaCl delivery to the macula densa is low).

Question 7

Which of the following should be present in the filtrate in the proximal convoluted tubule and used in co-transport?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Sodium ions

Sodium in the filtrate is cotransported with glucose and amino acids in the proximal tubule; cells and plasma proteins should not be in normal filtrate.

Question 8

Involuntary urination by a child after age 4 or 5, when bladder control is expected, is referred to as

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. enuresis.

Enuresis is involuntary urination after the age when bladder control is expected.

Question 9

What is the cause of most cases of pyelonephritis?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. An ascending infection by E. coli

Most pyelonephritis is an ascending infection, most often E. coli from the lower tract.

Question 10

Pyelonephritis may be distinguished from cystitis by the presence in pyelonephritis of

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. urinary casts and flank pain.

Urinary casts and flank pain point to kidney involvement (pyelonephritis); dysuria, urgency, and pyuria also occur in cystitis.

Question 11

Which pathophysiological process applies to acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Immune complexes deposit in glomerular tissue, causing inflammation.

Acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis is an immune-complex (type III) deposition in glomerular tissue.

Question 12

What causes the dark urine associated with acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Blood and protein leaking through the capillary into the filtrate

Dark “cola” urine comes from red cells and protein leaking through damaged glomeruli into the filtrate.

Question 13

Renal disease frequently causes hypertension because

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. congestion and ischemia stimulate release of renin.

Renal ischemia and congestion stimulate renin release, which raises blood pressure.

Question 14

Why does metabolic acidosis develop with bilateral kidney disease?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Tubule exchanges are impaired.

Diseased tubules cannot adequately secrete hydrogen ions or regenerate bicarbonate, so metabolic acidosis develops.

Question 15

What is the first indicator in the arterial blood gases of acidosis caused by glomerulonephritis?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Decreased bicarbonate ion

A falling bicarbonate level is the first typical arterial-blood-gas change of the metabolic acidosis of glomerulonephritis; pH may still be compensated.

Question 16

What would be the long-term effects of chronic infection or inflammation of the kidneys?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Gradual necrosis, fibrosis, and development of uremia, Kidney failure

Chronic infection or inflammation gradually produces necrosis, fibrosis, and eventually uremia and renal failure.

Question 17

What factors contribute to headache, anorexia, and lethargy with kidney disease?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

E. All of the above

Hypertension, uremia (elevated urea), anemia, and acidosis can all contribute to headache, anorexia, and lethargy.

Question 18

What are the significant signs of nephrotic syndrome?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Hyperlipidemia and lipiduria

Nephrotic syndrome features heavy proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, edema, hyperlipidemia, and lipiduria.

Question 19

Common causes of urolithiasis include all of the following EXCEPT

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. hyperlipidemia.

Hypercalcemia, hyperuricemia, and low fluid intake promote stones; hyperlipidemia is not a usual cause of urolithiasis.

Question 20

What does hydronephrosis lead to?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Ischemia and necrosis in the compressed area

Hydronephrosis compresses renal tissue, leading to ischemia and necrosis if unrelieved.

Question 21

Which of the following is a predisposing factor to bladder cancer?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Exposure to chemicals and cigarette smoke

Bladder cancer is associated with cigarette smoking and occupational chemical exposures (aromatic amines, etc.).

Question 22

What is the common initial sign of adenocarcinoma of the kidney?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Microscopic hematuria

The usual earliest sign of renal cell (adeno)carcinoma is microscopic hematuria; gross hematuria or pain often comes later.

Question 23

Which of the following does NOT usually result from nephrosclerosis?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Acute renal failure

Nephrosclerosis leads to ischemia, secondary hypertension, and chronic (not typically acute) renal failure.

Question 24

Which of the following relates to polycystic kidney disease?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. It results in gradual degeneration and chronic renal failure.

Adult polycystic kidney disease causes gradual cystic destruction of both kidneys and chronic renal failure.

Question 25

Which of the following is a cause for Wilms’ tumor?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. A genetic defect

Wilms tumor (nephroblastoma) is associated with genetic defects (for example WT1-related syndromes).

Question 26

With severe kidney disease can cause electrolyte imbalance and cause?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. cardiac arrhythmias and can lead to HF

Severe renal failure causes dangerous electrolyte shifts (especially hyperkalemia) that can trigger arrhythmias and heart failure.

Question 27

Which of the following indicates the early stage of acute renal failure?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Very low GFR and increased serum urea

The early oliguric phase of acute renal failure shows a very low GFR and a rising serum urea (azotemia).

Question 28

What is/are a cause(s) of acute tubule necrosis and acute renal failure?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. All of the above

Acute tubular necrosis and acute renal failure can follow prolonged shock, nephrotoxins, or massive crush injury/burns (myoglobinuria).

Question 29

Which of the following would likely cause chronic renal failure?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Diabetes

Diabetes mellitus is a major cause of chronic renal failure; a unilateral stone or a single episode of shock is less typical.

Question 30

What is the primary reason for hypocalcemia developing during end-stage renal failure or uremia?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. A deficit of activated vitamin D and hyperphosphatemia

In end-stage renal disease, deficient activated vitamin D and phosphate retention lower serum calcium.

Question 31

Cystitis is more common in females because

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. the urethra is short, wide, and adjacent to areas with resident flora.

The female urethra is short and close to perineal flora, which is why cystitis is more common in females.

Question 32

Which of the following is NOT likely to lead to hydronephrosis?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Nephrosclerosis

Nephrosclerosis is a vascular/ischemic process and does not obstruct urine outflow, so it does not cause hydronephrosis.

Question 33

Which of the following congenital defects is a common cause of cystitis in young children?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Vesicoureteral reflux

Vesicoureteral reflux lets bladder urine (and bacteria) go back to the kidney and is a common contributor to cystitis/UTI in children.

Question 34

Which of the following causes acute renal failure?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Bilateral acute glomerulonephritis

Bilateral acute glomerulonephritis can rapidly depress GFR and cause acute renal failure; the other listed conditions are more often chronic or unilateral.

Question 35

Uremic signs of renal failure include all of the following EXCEPT

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. high blood pressure.

Encephalopathy, osteodystrophy, azotemia, and acidosis are uremic features. Hypertension is common in renal failure but is not itself a uremic (waste-product) sign.

Question 36

Agenesis is often not diagnosed because

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. it is usually asymptomatic as one kidney provides adequate function

Unilateral renal agenesis is often unnoticed because the remaining kidney can provide adequate function.

Question 37

Wilms’ tumor is

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. an encapsulated mass in one kidney.

Wilms tumor is typically a large encapsulated malignant mass in one kidney of a young child.

Question 38

The micturition reflex is initiated by

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. increased pressure distending the bladder.

Stretch (increased pressure) in the bladder wall initiates the micturition reflex.

Question 39

In acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, the glomerular inflammation results from

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. a type III hypersensitivity reaction.

Post-streptococcal glomerular inflammation is a type III immune-complex hypersensitivity, not direct bacterial invasion of the kidney.

Question 40

Circulatory shock can affect the kidneys because it causes a decreases in blood pressure which leads to

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. decreased GFR and increased renin secretion.

A drop in blood pressure reduces GFR and stimulates renin secretion (and can progress to ischemic tubular injury if severe).

Question 41

The reabsorption of water and electrolytes by the kidneys is directly controlled by

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

G. 1, 2

ADH and atrial natriuretic hormone directly adjust water and sodium handling. Angiotensin acts mainly by stimulating aldosterone (more indirect), and bicarbonate level is not a primary controller.

Question 42

Uncontrolled essential hypertension may cause chronic renal failure because of

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. damage to afferent arterioles and renal ischemia.

Long-standing essential hypertension damages afferent arterioles, causing renal ischemia and eventually chronic renal failure.

Question 43

Excess urea and other nitrogen wastes in the blood is referred to as

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. azotemia.

Azotemia is excess urea and other nitrogenous wastes in the blood.

Question 44

Urethritis, infection/inflammation of the urethra, is classified into two categories:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. gonococcal and nongonococcal.

Urethritis is commonly classified as gonococcal or nongonococcal.
