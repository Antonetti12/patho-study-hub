Neuro / in-class practice for chapter 14. This test has no official instructor key, so a score is not calculated. Suggested answers stay hidden until you open “Show suggested answer” on a question.

Question 1

Which of the following is a TRUE statement about transient ischemic attacks (TIAs)?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. They can warn of potential cerebrovascular accidents

TIAs are brief ischemic events that resolve but warn of a possible stroke.

Question 2

One function of the somatic nervous system that is not performed by the autonomic nervous system is conduction of impulses:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. to skeletal muscles.

The somatic nervous system uniquely supplies skeletal muscle; the ANS supplies involuntary effectors.

Question 3

The composition of cerebrospinal fluid is

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. a plasma-like liquid with glucose, salts, and proteins

CSF is a clear, plasma-like fluid containing glucose and salts, with very little protein.

Question 4

Through what area does the cerebrospinal fluid circulate around the brain and spinal cord?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. In the subarachnoid space

CSF circulates in the subarachnoid space around the brain and cord.

Question 5

Neurons that carry impulses away from the CNS are called

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. efferent neurons.

Efferent (motor) neurons carry impulses away from the CNS.

Question 6

Which of the following applies to the corticospinal tract?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. It is a pyramidal tract for efferent impulses.

The corticospinal tract is a pyramidal motor (efferent) pathway.

Question 7

What is a major function of the limbic system?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Determines emotional responses

The limbic system is the main driver of emotional responses.

Question 8

What does a vegetative state refer to?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Loss of awareness and intellectual function but continued brainstem function

A vegetative state is loss of awareness and cognition with preserved brainstem function.

Question 9

Which of the following conditions is NOT part of the criteria for a declaration of “brain death”?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Presence of any head injury

Brain death requires no EEG activity, no reflexes, and no spontaneous respirations—not merely the presence of a head injury.

Question 10

What is the best definition of aphasia?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. The inability to comprehend or express language appropriately

Aphasia is impaired comprehension or expression of language; unclear articulation is dysarthria.

Question 11

Neurons are specialized for the conduction of impulses, whereas neuroglia

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. support nerve tissue.

Neuroglia support and protect neurons rather than conduct impulses.

Question 12

What is an early indicator of increased intracranial pressure?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Decreasing responsiveness

Decreasing responsiveness (level of consciousness) is an early sign of rising ICP; papilledema and fixed pupils come later.

Question 13

What is the typical change in blood pressure in a patient who has increased intracranial pressure?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Increasing pulse pressure

Cushing change is a widening pulse pressure (rising systolic, relatively low diastolic).

Question 14

The largest category of primary malignant brain tumors that arise from cells in the central nervous system (CNS) are called

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. gliomas.

Gliomas are the largest group of primary malignant CNS tumors.

Question 15

Which of the following causes papilledema?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Increased pressure of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) at the optic disc

Papilledema is swelling of the optic disc from transmitted high CSF pressure.

Question 16

The reticular activating system:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. maintains wakefulness.

The reticular activating system maintains wakefulness and alertness.

Question 17

What is the effect of an enlarging brain abscess on cardiovascular activity?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Systemic vasoconstriction and slower heart rate

Rising ICP triggers the Cushing response: systemic vasoconstriction and a slower heart rate.

Question 18

As intracranial pressure rises, the pupil of the eye, ipsilateral to the lesion, becomes dilated and unresponsive to light because of pressure on the

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. peripheral nervous system (PNS) fibers in cranial nerve III.

Ipsilateral pupil dilation comes from compression of parasympathetic fibers in CN III.

Question 19

The sympathetic division of the autonomic nervous system:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. Both A and B

Sympathetic outflow is thoracolumbar (T1–L2) and mobilizes energy for 'fight or flight.'

Question 20

The parasympathetic division of the autonomic nervous system:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Both a and b are correct.

Parasympathetic nerves conserve energy and have short postganglionic fibers; paravertebral ganglia are sympathetic.

Question 21

Which of the following statements is TRUE about malignant brain tumors?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Primary brain tumors rarely metastasize outside the CNS.

Primary brain tumors rarely spread outside the CNS.

Question 22

Why are focal or generalized seizures sometimes an early indication of a brain tumor?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Surrounding inflammation stimulates neurons to discharge spontaneously.

Irritation and inflammation around a tumor can make nearby neurons fire and produce seizures.

Question 23

What is the probable source of an embolus causing a cerebrovascular accident (CVA)?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Common carotid artery

An arterial embolus to the brain commonly originates in the carotid (or left heart)—not the venous system.

Question 24

Collateral circulation is most likely to be present when a CVA results from

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. atherosclerosis.

Slow atherosclerotic narrowing allows time for collateral circulation to develop.

Question 25

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. maximum necrosis and infarction develop within several hours of onset.

Infarction and deficits often evolve over 1–2 days, so 'maximum necrosis within several hours' is the exception.

Question 26

Signs and symptoms of a stroke depend upon

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. location of obstruction, size of artery, and area affected.

Stroke signs depend on the artery involved, its size, and the brain territory it supplies.

Question 27

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. decorticate posturing.

Decorticate posturing is flexion of the arms with extension of the legs.

Question 28

Cerebral death:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. permits normal internal homeostasis.

Cerebral (neocortical) death can leave the brainstem able to maintain basic internal homeostasis.

Question 29

Precipitating causes of seizure include all of the following except:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. hyperglycemia.

Hypoglycemia, not hyperglycemia, is the typical metabolic trigger for seizures; meningitis, stroke, and fever can precipitate them.

Question 30

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. tonic-clonic

A postictal sleep is typical after a tonic-clonic seizure.

Question 31

Alzheimer disease:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. is most prevalent as a late-onset dementia.

Alzheimer disease is most often late-onset; plaques and tangles also occur, but acetylcholine is decreased, not increased, so 'all of the above' is wrong.

Question 32

Which statement is not true regarding increasing intracranial pressures?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Accumulating CO2 causes vasoconstriction.

CO2 dilates, rather than constricts, cerebral vessels, so that statement is not true.

Question 33

Intellectual function is impaired in the dementing process. Which intellectual function is not impaired?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

E. e. All of the above functions are impaired

Dementia can impair new and old memory, abstraction, and language—so none of those functions is reliably spared.

Question 34

In an automobile accident, an individual’s forehead struck the windshield. The coup/contrecoup injury would be in the

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A forehead (coup) impact produces a frontal injury and a contrecoup injury at the occipital pole.

Question 35

Show suggested answer

E. e. diaphragmatic impairment

High cervical cord injury can paralyze the diaphragm (C3–C5) and stop breathing.

Question 36

Autonomic dysreflexia causes hypertension, not hypotension.

Best match: sudden focal deficits that clear without infarction (modern definitions usually limit TIA duration to under an hour, so the wording is slightly off).
