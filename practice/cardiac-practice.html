Cardiac Practice Test

Cardiac practice questions (week 7). This test has no official instructor key, so a score is not calculated. Suggested answers stay hidden until you open “Show suggested answer” on a question.

Official-key items are scored automatically after you submit. Suggested answers were written by the site owner’s study assistant, not the instructor. They stay hidden until you open “Show suggested answer” on that question and are not scored. Verify them against your notes. Incomplete items are left unscored.

Question 1

Septic shock is frequently caused by infections involving

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. gram-negative endotoxin-producing bacteria.

Septic shock is commonly triggered by gram-negative bacteria and their endotoxin.

Question 2

Excessive fluid in the pericardial space causes

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. reduced venous return.

Excess pericardial fluid (tamponade) limits filling and therefore reduces venous return and cardiac output.

Question 3

Varicose ulcers may develop and be slow to heal because

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. edema reduces arterial blood supply to the area.

Edema around varicose veins can impair arterial perfusion of the skin, so ulcers form and heal slowly.

Question 4

Prophylactic antibacterial drugs such as amoxicillin are given to patients with certain congenital heart defects or damaged heart valves immediately before invasive procedures to prevent

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. infectious endocarditis.

Periprocedural antibiotics are given to prevent infective endocarditis in patients with certain valve or congenital lesions.

Question 5

In a child with acute rheumatic fever, arrhythmias may develop due to the presence of

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. myocarditis.

In rheumatic fever, myocarditis is the lesion most likely to cause arrhythmias.

Question 6

In a child with ventricular septal defect, altered blood flow

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. is called a left-to-right shunt

A VSD usually shunts blood from the higher-pressure left ventricle to the right ventricle (left-to-right).

Question 7

In patients with congestive heart failure, ACE inhibitor drugs are useful because they

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. reduce renin and aldosterone secretion.

ACE inhibitors reduce angiotensin II and therefore reduce aldosterone (and the effects of renin–angiotensin signaling).

Question 8

Which of the following compensations that develop in patients with congestive heart failure eventually increase the workload of the heart?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

E. Faster heart rate and cardiomegaly, peripheral vasoconstriction, increased secretion of renin

Tachycardia, cardiomegaly, vasoconstriction, and renin–aldosterone activation all eventually increase the work of the failing heart.

Question 9

The right side of the heart would fail first in the case of

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

H. 3, 4

A large right-ventricular infarct or advanced COPD/pulmonary hypertension overloads the right side first; mitral stenosis and essential hypertension strain the left side first.

Question 10

A very rapid heart rate reduces cardiac output because

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. ventricular filling is reduced.

A very rapid rate shortens diastole, so ventricular filling and stroke volume fall.

Question 11

More extensive permanent damage is likely when a myocardial infarction is caused by

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. a thrombus.

An occlusive thrombus in situ is more likely to cause extensive, permanent infarction than a transient embolus or an arrhythmia alone.

Question 12

Heart block, in which a conduction delay at the AV node results in intermittent missed ventricular contractions, is called

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. second-degree block.

Second-degree AV block produces intermittent dropped ventricular beats.

Question 13

Septic shock differs from hypovolemic shock in that it is frequently manifested by

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. fever and flushed face.

Early (distributive) septic shock often presents with fever and warm, flushed skin, unlike cool, pale hypovolemic shock.

Question 14

Confirmation of the diagnosis of a myocardial infarction would include

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

F. 1, 3

MI is confirmed by characteristic ECG changes plus elevated cardiac biomarkers; pain pattern and nonspecific inflammation are less definitive.

Question 15

Which of the following is NOT true of the drug nitroglycerin?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. It strengthens the myocardial contraction

Nitroglycerin is a vasodilator that reduces workload; it does not strengthen contraction.

Question 16

Which of the following is considered to be the most dangerous arrhythmia?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Ventricular fibrillation

Ventricular fibrillation produces no effective cardiac output and is the most immediately lethal arrhythmia listed.

Question 17

A cardiac pacemaker would most likely be inserted in cases of

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. heart block.

A pacemaker is used for symptomatic or high-grade heart block; defibrillation (not a pacing indication here) treats ventricular fibrillation.

Question 18

A common adverse effect of many antihypertensive medications is

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. orthostatic hypotension.

Orthostatic hypotension is a common adverse effect of many antihypertensive drugs.

Question 19

The classic early manifestation(s) of left-sided congestive heart failure is/are ____, whereas the early indicator(s) of right-sided failure is/are _______.

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. shortness of breath on exertion or lying down; swelling of the ankles

Left-sided failure presents early with dyspnea on exertion or lying down; right-sided failure presents with peripheral edema such as ankle swelling.

Question 20

Shock develops in patients with severe burns as a result of

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. pain and loss of plasma.

Burn shock is driven by pain and massive plasma loss into injured tissues, not primarily by hemorrhage.

Question 21

With shock, anaerobic cell metabolism and decreased renal blood flow cause

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. metabolic acidosis.

Anaerobic metabolism and poor renal perfusion in shock produce metabolic acidosis.

Question 22

A prolonged period of shock is likely to cause

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. damage to, and increased permeability of, pulmonary capillaries.

Prolonged shock can injure pulmonary capillaries (shock lung/ARDS) and increase their permeability.

Question 23

Neurogenic (vasogenic) shock results from systemic vasodilation due to

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. increased capacity of the vascular system and reduced venous return

Neurogenic shock is loss of vasomotor tone, so vascular capacity rises and venous return falls.

Question 24

Why does anaphylactic shock cause severe hypoxia very quickly?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Bronchoconstriction and bronchial edema reduce airflow.

Anaphylaxis causes rapid hypoxia from bronchoconstriction and airway edema as well as vasodilation.

Question 25

A compensation for shock would include

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. increased heart rate and oliguria.

Compensated shock includes tachycardia and oliguria (sympathetic and renin–aldosterone effects).

Question 26

What are the early signs of circulatory shock?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

F. 1, 3

Early signs include pale moist skin and anxiety or restlessness. Loss of consciousness is late; a rapid pulse occurs early, but “strong” is only briefly true, and no option lists 1, 3, and 4 together.

Question 27

Shock follows a myocardial infarction when

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. a large portion of the myocardium is damaged.

Cardiogenic shock after MI occurs when a large portion of the myocardium is damaged and pump function collapses.

Question 28

An echocardiogram is used to demonstrate any abnormal

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. movement of the heart valves.

Echocardiography shows chamber and valve motion (and estimated flows); it is not the test of the conduction system itself.

Question 29

The term intermittent claudication refers to

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. ischemic muscle pain in the legs, particularly with exercise.

Intermittent claudication is ischemic leg-muscle pain, typically brought on by exercise and relieved by rest.

Question 30

Atherosclerosis in the iliac or femoral arteries is likely to cause which of the following?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

F. 1, 3

Iliac or femoral atherosclerosis can cause claudication and, when severe, ischemic or gangrenous ulcers; pulses are weakened, not strong.

Question 31

Uncontrolled hypertension is most likely to cause ischemia and loss of function in the

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. kidneys, brain, and retinas of the eye.

The key target organs of uncontrolled hypertension include the kidneys, brain, and retina.

Question 32

Pericarditis may be caused by

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

H. 1, 2, 3, 4

Pericarditis can follow infection, immune reactions, injury, or malignancy — all of the listed causes.

Question 33

Pericarditis causes a reduction in cardiac output as a result of which of the following?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Excess fluid in the pericardial cavity, which decreases ventricular filling

Pericardial effusion limits diastolic filling, so stroke volume and cardiac output fall.

Question 34

Septic emboli, a common complication of infective endocarditis, are a result of the fact that

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. vegetations are loosely attached and fragile.

Infective vegetations are fragile and can break off as septic emboli.

Question 35

The initial effect on the heart in cases of rheumatic fever is

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. acute inflammation in all layers of the heart due to abnormal immune response.

The initial cardiac injury in rheumatic fever is a pancarditis from an abnormal immune response, not direct streptococcal infection of the heart.

Question 36

Cyanosis occurs in children with tetralogy of Fallot because

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. a large amount of hemoglobin in the general circulation is unoxygenated.

Cyanosis appears when a large amount of unoxygenated hemoglobin reaches the systemic circulation.

Question 37

Unoxygenated blood enters the systemic circulation in children with tetralogy of Fallot because

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. pulmonary stenosis changes the ventricular pressures.

Pulmonary stenosis raises right-ventricular pressure, so blood shunts right-to-left through the VSD into the overriding aorta.

Question 38

Which of the following describes the blood flow occurring with a ventricular septal defect?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. From the left ventricle to the right ventricle

In a typical VSD, blood flows from the higher-pressure left ventricle into the right ventricle.

Question 39

A sign of aortic stenosis is

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. a heart murmur.

Aortic stenosis produces a characteristic systolic murmur; cardiac output tends to fall, not rise.

Question 40

Effects that may be expected from a beta-adrenergic blocking drug include

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. decreased sympathetic stimulation of the heart.

Beta blockers decrease sympathetic stimulation of the heart (and can reduce renin release).

Question 41

In an infant, the initial indication of congestive heart failure is often

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. feeding problems.

In infants, feeding difficulty (and poor weight gain or sweating with feeds) is often the first clue to heart failure.

Question 42

Which of the following drugs improves cardiac efficiency by slowing the heart rate and increasing the force of cardiac contractions?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Digoxin

Digoxin slows conduction/heart rate and increases the force of contraction.

Question 43

In which blood vessels will failure of the left ventricle cause increased hydrostatic pressure?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Pulmonary capillaries

Left-ventricular failure raises hydrostatic pressure in the pulmonary capillaries, causing pulmonary congestion or edema.

Question 44

Compensation mechanisms for decreased cardiac output in cases of congestive heart failure include

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. increased renin and aldosterone secretions.

Decreased cardiac output activates renin–angiotensin–aldosterone, which retains salt and water and is a major CHF compensation.

Question 45

Significant signs of right-sided congestive heart failure include

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. edematous feet and legs with hepatomegaly.

Right-sided failure causes systemic backup: leg edema and hepatomegaly. Frothy blood-streaked sputum is a left-sided (pulmonary edema) sign.
