Question 1

What is the result of one relaxing their external intercostal muscles and their diaphragm?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Air is forced out of the lungs.

Relaxing the diaphragm and external intercostals decreases thoracic volume and forces air out (expiration).

Question 2

The respiratory mucosa is continuous through the

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

I. 1, 2, 3, 4

Respiratory mucosa is continuous through the upper and lower tracts, sinuses, pharynx, and the auditory tube/middle ear.

Question 3

The maximum volume of air a person can exhale after a maximum inspiration is termed the

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. vital capacity.

Vital capacity is the maximum volume exhaled after a maximum inspiration.

Question 4

Which of the following applies to the blood in the pulmonary artery?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. PO2 is low.

Pulmonary-artery blood is venous blood going to the lungs, so PO2 is low; hydrostatic pressure there is not high.

Question 5

Which of the following causes bronchodilation?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Epinephrine

Epinephrine stimulates β2 receptors and dilates bronchi; histamine, parasympathetic input, and β2 blockers do the opposite.

Question 6

The central chemoreceptors in the medulla are normally most sensitive to

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. elevated carbon dioxide level.

Central chemoreceptors respond primarily to elevated PaCO2 (via CSF pH/H+).

Question 7

Oxygen diffuses from the alveoli to the blood because

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. PO2 is lower in the blood.

Oxygen diffuses down its pressure gradient because alveolar PO2 is higher than blood PO2.

Question 8

Carbon dioxide is primarily transported in the blood

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. as bicarbonate ion.

Most carbon dioxide is carried as bicarbonate ion.

Question 9

With respiratory alkalosis (compensated or decompensated) the level of this always decreased:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. PaCO2

Respiratory alkalosis is caused by a low PaCO2, so PaCO2 is always decreased; bicarbonate falls only after renal compensation.

Question 10

Carbaminohemoglobin is CO2 bound to amino groups on the globin chains, not to heme iron.

Question 11

Show suggested answer

C. rales.

Light, bubbly crackles from serous secretions are rales (crackles).

Question 12

B. Viral infection in child, 3 months to 3 years

Laryngotracheobronchitis (croup) is typically viral and most common in children about 3 months to 3 years old.

Question 13

Infectious rhinitis begins with watery discharge, congestion, and sneezing, not purulent drainage.

Antigenic drift or shift means immunity from a prior influenza infection may not protect against a new strain.

Question 15

Influenza is the most common cause of viral pneumonia; H. influenzae and pneumococcus are bacteria.

Question 16

Alveolar congestion and exudate impair oxygen diffusion and cause hypoxia.

Question 17

Rust-colored sputum is classically associated with Streptococcus pneumoniae (pneumococcal) pneumonia.

Question 18

A, B, and C are true of TB (reactivation when resistance falls, caseous tubercles, acid-fast bacillus not limited to the lungs). D overstates the role of HIV; TB is not only an opportunistic HIV infection.

Question 19

Histoplasmosis is a fungal infection.

Question 20

Cystic fibrosis is autosomal recessive and affects exocrine glands.

Question 21

Growth and development delays of a child with cystic fibrosis is due to?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. mucus plugs obstructing the flow of pancreatic enzymes.

Thick mucus blocks pancreatic ducts, so digestive enzymes do not reach the intestine and growth is delayed.

Question 22

Persistent thick mucus in the bronchioles of a child with cystic fibrosis may cause

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

Air trapping, atelectasis, repeated infections, and irreversible lung damage (1–4)

Thick bronchiolar mucus causes air trapping, atelectasis, repeated infections, and irreversible lung damage (1–4). “Black meconium” is not a bronchiole effect, and no listed combo is 1–4 alone.

Question 23

Hypercalcemia can be a paraneoplastic effect of PTH-like hormone (PTHrP) secretion by some lung tumors.

Question 24

Show suggested answer

Post-procedure aspiration often follows vomiting from drugs or anesthesia while airway-protective reflexes are depressed.

During an acute attack, obstruction comes from mucosal edema and thick mucus (and bronchoconstriction), not from muscle relaxation.

Question 26

Barrel chest in emphysema reflects air trapping, hyperinflation, and an increased residual volume.

Question 27

Which statement or statements apply to emphysema?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A, B, and D

In emphysema the gas-exchange surface is reduced, elastic-fiber breakdown may be genetic (α1-antitrypsin), and expiration is impaired. V/Q does not stay constant, and no combo option is listed.

Question 28

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Chronic irritation, inflammation, and recurrent infection of the larger airways

Chronic bronchitis is chronic irritation, inflammation, and recurrent infection of the larger airways, usually from smoking.

Question 29

Why does cor pulmonale develop with chronic pulmonary disease?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Pulmonary fibrosis and vasoconstriction increase vascular resistance.

Pulmonary fibrosis and hypoxic vasoconstriction raise pulmonary vascular resistance and strain the right ventricle (cor pulmonale).

Question 30

Restrictive disorders are grouped by impaired expansion from chest-wall or anatomic problems, or from lung-tissue damage.

Question 31

Inhaled silica and similar dusts cause pulmonary fibrosis and loss of compliance (pneumoconiosis).

Question 32

Severe Hypoxia is caused by Pulmonary Edema in patients due to?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. interference with expansion of the lungs.

Edema fluid makes the lungs stiffer and interferes with expansion and oxygen transfer, producing severe hypoxia.

Question 33

Which of the following is or are a cause of pulmonary edema?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A, B, and C

Left-sided heart failure, volume overload, and inhaled toxins can cause pulmonary edema. Hyperproteinemia would raise oncotic pressure and does not cause edema; no combo option is listed.

Question 34

What is a large-sized pulmonary embolus likely to cause?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Hypotension and right-sided heart failure

A large pulmonary embolus can obstruct outflow, drop systemic pressure, and cause acute right-sided heart failure.

Question 35

Show suggested answer

C. Air is absorbed from the alveoli distal to the obstruction.

Air distal to a complete airway obstruction is absorbed, and those alveoli collapse (absorption atelectasis).

Question 36

Show suggested answer

B and D

Flail chest is several ribs fractured at two sites; paradoxical motion can impair venous return and cardiac output. A describes an open chest wound, and tracheal/mediastinal shift toward the unaffected side is more typical of tension pneumothorax.

A simple closed pneumothorax typically causes uneven chest movement; tracheal deviation away from the lesion suggests tension pneumothorax.

A large open (“sucking”) pneumothorax lets the mediastinum flutter and impairs venous return.
