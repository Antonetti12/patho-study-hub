Question 1

Question 2

What type of bone cell is responsible for bone resorption?

Suggested · not an official instructor key

Osteoclasts resorb bone; osteoblasts form bone.

Question 3

What is the function of synovial fluid?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

Synovial fluid lubricates the joint and reduces friction.

Question 4

Show suggested answer

A greenstick fracture is an incomplete break with one side of the bone bent, common in children.

Question 5

What type of muscular dystrophy is most common in children?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Duchenne’s

Duchenne muscular dystrophy is the most common type in children.

Question 6

Osteoporosis results in:

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Bone resorption exceeding formation

In osteoporosis, bone resorption exceeds bone formation, so mass and density fall.

Question 7

What is the primary cause of gout?

Show suggested answer

B. Uric acid crystal deposits

Gout is caused by uric acid crystal deposits in joints.

Question 8

Osteoarthritis most often affects weight-bearing joints such as the knees and hips.

Question 9

B. severe headache, nuchal rigidity, and photophobia

A ruptured cerebral aneurysm often presents with a sudden severe headache, nuchal rigidity, and photophobia.

Question 10

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. transient muscle weakness in a hand or leg

A TIA causes brief, reversible deficits such as transient weakness in a hand or leg; consciousness is usually preserved.

Question 11

Hemorrhagic strokes generally have the poorest prognosis of the CVA types listed.

Question 12

After the primary injury, secondary brain damage commonly comes from hematoma, infection, ischemia, or swelling.

Question 13

Question 14

Show suggested answer

Secondary (metastatic) brain tumors most often come from lung or breast cancer.

Question 15

A vegetative state is loss of awareness and higher function with continued brainstem function.

Question 16

Question 17

Show suggested answer

Pain from damaged peripheral nerves is neuropathic pain.

Question 18

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

Migraines are associated with abnormal cerebral blood flow and neurovascular/metabolic changes.

Question 19

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

Tension headaches are commonly related to muscle tension from emotional stress.

Question 20

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

Myelinated A-delta fibers transmit sharp, acute pain; unmyelinated C fibers carry dull, chronic pain.

Documentable physiologic signs of acute pain include an elevated heart rate and blood pressure.

Question 22

Endorphins are endogenous opioids that block or reduce pain transmission.

Question 23

A withdrawal reflex produces involuntary movement away from a painful stimulus.

Question 24

Show suggested answer

Somatic pain arises from skin, bone, or muscle; visceral pain arises from organs.
