Question 5

Which of the following cells in the gastric mucosa produce intrinsic factor and hydrochloric acid?

Parietal cells secrete hydrochloric acid and intrinsic factor.

Question 6

When highly acidic chyme enters the duodenum, which hormone stimulates the release of pancreatic secretions that contains very high bicarbonate ion content?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Secretin

Secretin stimulates a bicarbonate-rich pancreatic secretion that buffers acidic chyme in the duodenum.

Question 7

In which structure is oxygenated blood (arterial) mixed with unoxygenated blood (venous) so as to support the functions of the structure?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Liver

In the liver, oxygenated hepatic-artery blood mixes with nutrient-rich portal venous blood to supply hepatocytes.

Question 8

Which of the following stimulates increased peristalsis and secretions in the digestive tract?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Vagus nerve

Parasympathetic (vagus) stimulation increases peristalsis and digestive secretions; sympathetic stimulation inhibits them.

Question 9

Which of the following is contained in pancreatic exocrine secretions?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Bicarbonate ion

Pancreatic exocrine juice is rich in bicarbonate; digestive enzymes are secreted in inactive form, and insulin is endocrine.

Question 10

The presence of food in the intestine stimulates intestinal activity but inhibits gastric activity through the

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. enterogastric reflex.

The enterogastric reflex lets intestinal filling slow gastric emptying and gastric activity.

Question 11

What does the term gluconeogenesis refer to?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Formation of glucose from protein and fat

Gluconeogenesis is formation of glucose from noncarbohydrate precursors such as protein and fat.

Question 12

The early stage of vomiting causes

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. metabolic alkalosis.

Early vomiting loses gastric acid and tends to cause metabolic alkalosis.

Question 13

Severe vomiting can lead to metabolic acidosis because of increased

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. hypovolemia and lactic acid production.

Prolonged severe vomiting causes hypovolemia and lactic acid production, which can shift the picture to metabolic acidosis.

Question 14

What does the defecation reflex require?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Coordination through the sacral spinal cord

The defecation reflex is coordinated through the sacral spinal cord (and requires relaxation of the external sphincter to void).

Question 15

What does congenital esophageal atresia cause?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. No fluid or food entering the stomach

Esophageal atresia is a blind-ending esophagus, so fluid and food cannot enter the stomach.

Question 16

Which of the following applies to cleft palate?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. The hard and soft palates do not fuse during the first trimester of pregnancy.

Cleft palate occurs when the palatine processes fail to fuse in the first trimester.

Question 17

Oral candidiasis is considered to

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. be an opportunistic fungal infection of the mouth.

Oral candidiasis is an opportunistic fungal (yeast) infection; the white plaques can usually be scraped off.

Question 18

There is no cure for herpes, and it tends to reoccur because?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. The virus persists in latent form in sensory nerve ganglia.

Herpes simplex persists in a latent form in sensory nerve ganglia and can reactivate.

Question 19

What does the term periodontitis refer to?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Bacterial damage to the teeth and surrounding alveolar bone

Periodontitis is bacterial destruction involving the teeth and supporting alveolar bone, deeper than simple gingivitis.

Question 20

A viral infection of the parotid gland is commonly known as

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. mumps.

Viral infection of the parotid gland is mumps.

Question 21

The telescoping of one section of bowel inside another section is called

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. intussusceptions.

Intussusception is telescoping of one bowel segment into another; volvulus is twisting.

Question 22

A. at the head of the pancreas.

A tumor at the head of the pancreas can obstruct bile or pancreatic flow early, so it may be found sooner.

Question 23

Show suggested answer

B. Most cases involve an adenocarcinoma of the mucous glands.

Most gastric cancers are adenocarcinomas of the mucous glands and are often found late.

Question 24

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. periodic flow of gastric contents into the esophagus.

GERD is periodic reflux of gastric contents into the esophagus.

Question 25

Which factor(s) appear(s) to have a role in the etiology of inflammatory bowel diseases?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Genetic and immunological factors

Inflammatory bowel disease has a strong genetic and immune-mediated component; a single dietary or toxic cause is not established.

Question 26

Although many factors may precipitate pancreatitis, the two major causes are

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

D. gallstones and alcohol abuse

The two major causes of acute pancreatitis are gallstones and alcohol abuse.

Question 27

At what stage of alcoholic liver disease can the damage be reversed?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

B. Initial stage

The initial fatty-liver stage of alcoholic liver disease can be reversed with abstinence; cirrhosis cannot.

Question 28

How does serum bilirubin change with cirrhosis?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Increased conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin

In cirrhosis both conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin typically rise because uptake, conjugation, and excretion are impaired.

Question 29

Identify a major reason making it difficult to prevent the spread of hepatitis B.

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

C. Infection is often asymptomatic.

Hepatitis B is often asymptomatic, which makes it hard to identify and prevent spread; a vaccine is available.

Question 30

Show suggested answer

F. 4 only

Jaundice commonly occurs with hepatocellular carcinoma. Hepatitis usually has a preicteric prodrome and is not always jaundiced, so “first manifestation” and “always jaundiced” are not reliable.

Question 31

Which of the following is/are related to post-hepatic jaundice?

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Pruritic skin and light-colored stools

Post-hepatic (obstructive) jaundice causes pale stools, dark urine, and pruritus from accumulated bile salts.

Question 32

Show suggested answer

Esophageal varices reflect portal hypertension from chronic liver disease, not ordinary icteric viral hepatitis.

Question 33

C. Cholangitis

Cholangitis is inflammation, usually infection, of the bile ducts.

Question 34

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

A. Hypoglycemia

Late dumping, about 2–3 hours after a meal, causes hypoglycemia from a rapid insulin response to a carbohydrate load.

Question 35

Smoked or nitrate-preserved foods and genetic factors predispose to gastric cancer; NSAIDs/ASA are ulcer risk factors, not typical gastric-carcinoma risks.

Hematemesis in peptic ulcer disease usually means a vessel has been eroded; perforation presents as an acute abdomen.

Peptic ulcers occur most often in the proximal duodenum.

In PID, infection can travel through the open fallopian tubes directly into the peritoneal cavity.

Bile leaking from a perforated gallbladder causes chemical peritonitis, which is often followed by bacterial peritoneal infection.

Question 41

A. Continued vomiting and fluid shift into the intestine

Obstruction causes ongoing vomiting and a third-space fluid shift into the bowel, producing hypovolemic shock.

Hirschsprung disease is congenital absence of parasympathetic ganglion cells in a segment of colon, so motility fails.

Colon cancer usually metastasizes first to the liver via portal venous drainage.

In Crohn disease, recurrent inflammation, necrosis, and fibrosis can create a fistula between loops of bowel or to other organs.

Crohn disease causes skip lesions: inflamed segments, often in the ileum, alternating with fibrotic or normal bowel.

Steatorrhea is bulky, fatty, foul-smelling stool from fat malabsorption.

Question 53

Activated pancreatic enzymes inflame the peritoneum and increase vascular permeability, so fluid is lost and shock can develop.

D. Autodigestion of tissue by pancreatic enzymes

Acute pancreatitis is autodigestion of pancreatic and surrounding tissue by prematurely activated pancreatic enzymes.

Question 55

Question 56

Show suggested answer

Suggested · not an official instructor key

Ascites in cirrhosis comes from portal hypertension, low plasma albumin (low oncotic pressure), and secondary hyperaldosteronism.

Question 57

Bleeding tendency in cirrhosis is largely from impaired hepatic synthesis of clotting factors, including those that require vitamin K.

Question 58

Esophageal varices form because portal hypertension raises hydrostatic pressure in portosystemic collateral veins.

Question 59

C. A small, firm, nodular liver and portal hypertension

The third (end) stage of alcoholic liver disease is cirrhosis: a small, firm, nodular liver with portal hypertension.

Question 60
